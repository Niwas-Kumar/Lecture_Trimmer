1. PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS AND USER PREFERENCES

The tool takes long teaching videos and produces three selectable study videos:

* Question solving: question statements, subparts, solution steps, final answers,
  exercise-related doubts, and theory needed to understand the solution.
* Theory: topics, definitions, formulas, derivations, proofs, conceptual doubts,
  and illustrative examples used to explain a concept.
* Theory + questions: both categories in the original lecture order.

Remove nonacademic material: motivation, greetings, unrelated chatter, homework
submission polls, leaderboards, platform logistics, announcements, technical
interruptions, and closing discussion. Actual homework problem solutions and
academic doubts must remain.

Short theory explanations between solution steps must stay with the question.
Do not turn theory or question videos into disconnected clips lasting a few
seconds. Preserve complete teaching context and smooth cuts.

Support different subjects, teachers, teaching styles, Hindi, English, and
Hinglish. Processing 2–4-hour lectures should be reasonably fast, but speed must
not compromise essential teaching content. Do not force a target retention
percentage or duration.

The user wants a shareable Windows EXE, private Google Sheet authentication,
an Update button, a compact attractive interface, logo/icon, Stop processing,
visible progress, useful output accuracy, simple user errors, and detailed
private diagnostics for support.

Current explicit speech preference: GROQ WHISPER LARGE V3 TURBO.
Classification remains Gemini through the PW proxy.

5. DATA FLOW AND CLASSIFICATION ARCHITECTURE

Google login / PW access verification
  -> upload ownership and immutable source snapshot
  -> FFmpeg audio extraction
  -> speech recognition and cached transcript
  -> downloadable raw timestamped transcript files
  -> complete teaching-activity map and passage review
  -> academic/nonacademic edits and dependency-aware category ranges
  -> shared section rendering
  -> three outputs, analysis report, timings, and AI usage

Classification primarily uses transcript text. Optional selected-frame review
adds bounded visual evidence for uncertain teaching sections. It is not
continuous understanding of the entire video. Static board content alone does
not establish that the teacher's current speech is academic.

Current classification version: lessons-v5.2-study-only.
Current rendering version seen in reports: render-v4-pcm.

The classifier now reasons about complete teaching activities and reviews
passages within them. It should remove chatter inside a question without losing
the related theory. Unresolved activity/category conflicts require correction
or review instead of automatically publishing them in both category videos.
Silent board work inside genuine teaching remains protected. That protection
must not retain an entire poll or closing announcement.

Older analysis caches are invalidated by prompt/version changes. Transcription
can be reused independently when its source and speech settings match.

6. PERFORMANCE, PROGRESS, STOP, AND MEDIA HANDLING

Implemented immediate processing status, elapsed time, live transcription-section
progress, real FFmpeg progress, and independently delivered finished videos.
Failure of one export does not hide other completed videos.

Sources are snapshotted to protect against changing uploads. Cache keys include
source identity and relevant processing settings. Cloud audio sections survive
retries. Shared source sections are rendered once and reused across categories.
Verified completed exports can also be reused.

Encoder selection tests NVIDIA NVENC, then Intel Quick Sync, then CPU libx264.
Hardware failures can fall back to CPU. Precise cuts use frame alignment,
boundary padding, and short audio fades. PCM intermediates and final AAC
encoding avoid accumulated audio timing drift. Audio continuity and timing are
validated. Fast keyframe cuts fall back to precise cuts when they would restore
excluded speech.

Stop processing propagates a per-job cancellation context through worker
threads and FFmpeg. Completed work is retained. An external request already
in flight can finish and incur a charge; Stop prevents further sections from
starting. Job ownership and update locks must remain enforced.

No universal processing-time promise exists. Duration, resolution, output
selection, provider quotas, network and hardware all affect total runtime.
